## THE ONE PLACE THE UPDATED DOCUMENT PULLS AGAINST ITSELF

Section 4 now asks for before-and-after imagery and describes what it wants as "a simple side-by-side
of a forward fold or a seated posture". Appendix A's pre-flight still requires that imagery is "free of
before-and-after body shots implying medical improvement". Asked to judge whether those two can
coexist: **yes, and the distinction is real, but it is narrow and it lives entirely in the execution.**

A range-of-movement side-by-side shows a joint angle. A before-and-after body shot shows a body. The
first is a demonstration of what somebody can now do, the second is an implied health outcome, and the
category's exposure sits on the second. So the side-by-side runs only under four conditions, and if any
one of them fails it becomes the thing the pre-flight rules out:

1. **Same clothing, same camera position, same lighting, both frames dated.** A difference produced by
   angle or light is a manufactured difference, which is a separate problem from the compliance one.
2. **Fully clothed, in loose kit, with the joint as the subject rather than the torso.** The moment the
   body reads as the subject, it is a body shot regardless of what the caption says.
3. **The caption names the movement and the case-file timeline, never a health outcome.** "Forward
   fold, week one and week twelve" runs. "Twelve weeks to a better back" does not.
4. **The results-vary note sits adjacent**, not in the footer.

This is the one judgement in the build where I would want the client's own legal comfort before it
ships, because it is the only place the document's own two sections point in different directions.

## FAQ (6 items, sits between beat 13 and beat 14)
**Carries: E · Reassure**

**H2:** Fair questions.

**"I have tried yoga. It did nothing, or it made it worse."**
Probably a flow class, and a flow class is not sequenced for a back that has been compensating for two years. The wrong shape on an irritated spine sets it backwards. This is a sequenced method with a teacher watching, and the first phase deliberately does not go near the part that hurts.

**"The NHS and my physio already do this."**
This is not a replacement for physiotherapy, for your GP, or for any clinical care you are having. It sits alongside them. What five live days add is a specific sequence, a teacher watching how you move in real time, and a method you keep and run yourself afterwards.

**"My employer gives me an app for this for free."**
Then use it. What it cannot do is watch you. App content is asynchronous and nobody is correcting the way you are actually performing the movement. We are not a regulated medical device and we do not claim to be one. We are a live teacher, in a room, five days running.

**"What if I cannot make the live session?"**
Every session runs twice, morning and evening, with the same content, so you pick one. If you miss one entirely there is a replay for 24 hours. It expires after that, because the useful part is the correction and the questions, and an open replay library turns this into content nobody watches.

**"Do I need to be flexible, or buy anything?"**
No, and no. A mat, a wall, a belt or a dressing gown cord, and a couple of cushions. The one-page list is sent when you register. Props are how a body that cannot yet do the shape gets the benefit of it safely.

**"Is five days really going to change anything?"**
Honestly: five days is not where the change lives, and we would rather tell you that now than sell you something else. The case files say the first real change usually lands around the third session, three weeks for morning stiffness and for headaches easing, eight weeks for a full working day seated. What the five days give you is your own number on Monday and again on Thursday, and a clear read on whether this is worth your time.
