**Deliberately NOT created as bonuses**, and each for a documented reason:
- Any recorded practice, sequence or exercise video. Section 4 rules this out on clinical grounds and

- Anything from the 250-hour library. Its place is the prescription call or the end of the six months.
- Any bonus carrying a stated pound value. Appendix A: the pound values come off, the contents stay on.

**What these four are really for.** They are listed on the page as value, and they are. Operationally
they are **show-up devices**. Section 5 sets day-one show-up at above 45 per cent of registrants and
escalates below 30, and it is the metric the whole funnel hangs off. A registrant who has scored a
number, gathered a belt and two cushions and read one page that told them something nobody else had is
a registrant who turns up. Every one of the four ends on the start date and the session times for that
reason.

### PAGE 1

**Why we ask you for a number**

Most people describe how they are doing in adjectives. Bad week. Not too bad. Manageable. Adjectives
move around, they follow your mood and your Tuesday, and four days later nobody can tell whether
anything shifted.

A number holds still.

So before the first session on Monday, you are going to write five numbers down. On Thursday, after
day four, you are going to write five more. Then you compare them.

The important part is that they are yours. Nobody at SuperMe is going to tell you what changed. You
will be reading your own handwriting from Monday.

**How to use this sheet**

1. Fill in the Monday column **before** the first session starts, not after it. Ten minutes before is
   ideal, while the day still feels like a normal day.
2. Score each moment out of ten, where 0 is "nothing at all" and 10 is "as bad as it has been".
3. Fold the page over, or put it somewhere you will not read it again until Thursday.
4. After day four, fill in the Thursday column **without looking at Monday first**. Then open it up.

If a moment on the list does not apply to you, leave it blank rather than guessing. A blank is more
useful to you on Thursday than an invented number.

**A note on what this is**

This is a self-report sheet for your own use across five days. It is not a clinical measure, it is not
a diagnosis, and it is not a substitute for anything your GP or clinician has told you.

### PAGE 1 · WHY THIS PAGE EXISTS

Two people describe the same thing. The stairs have become something they think about before they do
them. Getting up off the floor has become a small production. Neither of them mentions it at work.

They can be describing two completely different situations, and the answer is not the same for both.

Almost nobody gets told which one they are. They get told to rest it, or to strengthen it, or that it
is their age. So they either do nothing, or they do the wrong one energetically and it gets angrier.

This page sets out the two, plainly, including the part that most of this industry will not put in
writing.

We are not diagnosing you here. Which of the two you have is a question for your GP or your clinician,
and it is worth actually asking them.

---

### PAGE 2 · THE FIRST KNEE: LOAD

**What it is.** Upper body weight going through the joint, day after day. Stairs. Standing. Walking.
Years of it. The muscles that are supposed to share that load, the thighs, the glutes and the calves,
have quietly stopped taking their share, so more and more of it lands on the joint itself. The joint
complains. The complaining makes the person use it less. Using it less weakens the muscles further.

**What it responds to.** Taking the load off the joint, then building the structures above it and below
it so they carry what they are supposed to carry.

**What Atul does with it.** This is the part people find strange when they first hear it. With a knee
like that, he would not bend the knee at all for the first few weeks. He would build the muscles above
and below it, and take the load off the joint, and only then go near the movement that hurts. Same
sequence as a back: unload, brace, then move.

**The honest timeline.** From the case file. Female, 40. Weak quadriceps, tight IT bands, poor weight
distribution, stairs and sitting on the floor becoming difficult. Twelve weeks to the first real
decrease in pain on the stairs, at twenty-four sessions. Eighteen to twenty weeks to functional
stability. She takes a full group class now without modifications.

Twelve weeks is not a headline. It is the truth, and it is the reason the work after the challenge is
counted in months rather than weeks. Individual results vary.

---

### PAGE 3 · THE SECOND KNEE: WEAR

**What it is.** Change in the joint surfaces themselves, over years. A clinician diagnoses this, not a
yoga teacher and not a webpage.

**What we will not tell you.** That it goes away. It does not, and anyone selling you a belt, a powder
or a six-week programme on the promise that it will is selling you something they cannot deliver.

**What regular practice does do.** People who practise consistently stop noticing it in ordinary life,
and feel it after they have overdone something. That is a real outcome and a valuable one. It is
control of the day to day rather than a different joint.

**Why we put the limit in writing.** Because everything else in this category promises the same thing to
everyone, and a promise that covers everyone covers nobody. Telling you plainly which outcome is
available is the only way the rest of what we say is worth anything.

**What to do with this page**

Ask your GP or clinician which of the two describes you. If you have had imaging, the answer already
exists in a letter somewhere. If you have not, the history usually tells a lot: how it started, whether
it is worse first thing or worse after use, and whether it swells.

Then come to day one. Knees are served in the same session as backs and necks, through level and prop
variations, because the sequence is the same and only the structures change.

### PAGE 1

In March 2020 a lot of people put a laptop on a kitchen table, said it would be a few weeks, and are
still there. The chair came from the dining set. The screen is the laptop's own, which means it sits
about thirty centimetres below where your eyes want it. Nine hours a day, five days a week, for years.

None of that is anybody's fault and none of it was a decision. It was a Tuesday in a strange spring.

But the desk is upstream of most of what a working body complains about, and it is the one thing in
this list you can change before the first session without moving at all.

**The six.**

**1 · The top of the screen at eye level.**
Not the middle. The top. If you are looking down at a laptop all day, the head sits forward of the
shoulders and the neck and upper back carry it. A stack of books under the laptop does the same job as
a stand.

**2 · The screen about an arm's length away.**
Reach out. Your fingertips should roughly touch it. Closer than that and people lean in without
noticing.

**3 · A separate keyboard and mouse, if the laptop is raised.**
The moment you lift the screen, the keyboard goes too high. Any cheap external keyboard sorts this. It
is the single most common thing missing from a home set-up.

**4 · Feet flat, knees around the same height as the hips or a little below.**
If the chair is too high, a couple of books under the feet. If it is too low, a folded blanket on the
seat. Neither costs anything.

**5 · Something behind the lower back.**
A rolled towel or a small cushion in the gap. Most dining chairs are flat and most people end up
resting against nothing at all.

**6 · The phone, held up rather than down.**
This is the neck one. Reading a phone at eye level and reading it resting in your lap are not the same
load going through the neck, and it is the difference between a few minutes and a few hours a day
compounding over years. Bring the phone up rather than taking the head down.

A corporate professional came to Atul after two years of afternoon headaches, which she had been
calling headaches. Three weeks and eight sessions later they had eased and her range had come back. The
headache at four o'clock and the desk are usually the same story, and almost nobody connects them.
Individual results vary.

**And the one that beats all six.**

No arrangement of furniture is a substitute for changing position. The best chair in the world is
still a chair. Get up between meetings rather than between hours.

> # What to have beside your mat
> ### Nothing on this list needs buying. That is the point.

**Props are not a beginner's crutch.** They are how you tell a nervous system that it is allowed to let
go. A body that cannot yet do a shape gets the whole benefit of it safely with a wall and a belt, and
taking the load off is the entire first phase of the Inner Brace Method. If you have ever been in a
class where props were handed out apologetically, this is not that.

Gather these before Monday and day one starts on time.

**1 · Something to lie on.**
A yoga mat if you own one. A rug, or a folded blanket on a hard floor, if you do not. Nobody is buying
a mat for this.

**2 · A clear stretch of wall.**
About a metre wide, with nothing hanging on it. The wall does a surprising amount of the work in the
first two days.

**3 · A belt, or a dressing gown cord.**
A long strap of some kind, roughly the length of your arm span. A luggage strap or a scarf works. This
is how you reach something your hands cannot yet reach, without straining to get there.

**4 · Two cushions, or two yoga blocks.**
Firm rather than soft. Sofa cushions are usually too squashy. Two thick books wrapped in a tea towel
are genuinely fine.

**5 · A folded blanket.**
For height and for padding, and for the end of the session.

**6 · A sturdy chair.**
Dining chair rather than an office chair, so it does not roll or swivel. Some of the standing work on
day four uses one for support.

**And two things that are not props.**

**Space.** Enough room to lie down and take your arms overhead without hitting anything. Most people
end up in the living room rather than the spare bedroom.

**A camera he can see you through.** This is the whole reason the sessions are live rather than
recorded. Prop the laptop or phone so that all of you is in the frame, standing and lying down, not
just your head. Atul corrects what he can see. If he cannot see you, he cannot correct you, and you
have paid for the one thing an application cannot do.

**Joining**

Your Zoom link is in your confirmation email and goes out again on the morning of day one. Join five
minutes early and set your name to the one you would like to be called.

## COMPLIANCE READ ON ALL FOUR PDFs

Every one of them was written under the same line as the landing page, because a cold buyer reads them
before they have met Atul.

- **Nothing performs a movement.** Not one of the four contains an instruction to get into a position or
  hold one. Score Card is an instrument, Two Knees is an explanation, Desk Sheet is furniture, Prop
  Sheet is a shopping list you do not shop for. The one place a full version would have crossed that
  line, a "how to tell which knee you have" self-check, I stopped and flagged rather than wrote.
- **No banned verbs and no "pain-free".** Checked line by line.
- **No condition attributed to the reader in the second person.** The Score Card asks the reader to score
  their own moments, which is self-report, not attribution. The Two Knees describes two categories and
  routes the reader to a clinician for which one is theirs.
- **No invented numbers.** No load figures on the phone line, no interval on the get-up line, no
  percentage anywhere. Only case-file timelines, with the results-vary note attached.
- **No pound values on any of them**, on the page or inside the PDFs.
- **Every one carries a footer disclaimer** and the date block.

## WHAT THE UPDATED STRATEGY CHANGED IN THESE FOUR

Deliberately small. The update is entirely about proof, and three of the four PDFs are not proof assets,
so most of this set needed nothing. Two changes were warranted and made:

1. **The Score Card gained a fifth row**, for the headache or the shoulder by the end of the working
   day. This is the one that mattered. The neck segment is a quarter of the audience, it is now proven,
   and the sheet it scores itself on had no row it could honestly use. A neck buyer filling in a
   four-row sheet built around mornings, floors and sleep would have found the instrument did not fit
   them, on day one, which is the worst possible moment to discover it. The row carries "leave it blank
   if it is not one of yours" so it never prompts a symptom into existence.
2. **The Desk Sheet's phone item gained the neck proof story.** It was already the neck item on the
   sheet, and it was making the connection without any evidence behind it. It now carries the corporate
   professional's real timeline, third person, with the results-vary note.

**The Two Knees is unchanged** apart from an angle-number reference, because the update added nothing to
the knee file. **What to Have Beside Your Mat is unchanged**, because a prop list does not vary by which
pain someone arrived with.
